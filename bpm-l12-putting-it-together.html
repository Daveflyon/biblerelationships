Putting It Together – Bible Pointers on Marriage

Lesson 12 of 12

Opening Question

Please begin by answering this question honestly in your own words.

Looking back at all eleven lessons, which ones have shifted something in how you think about marriage? What drew you to it?

Key Scripture

Joshua 1:8

"This Book of the Law shall not depart from your mouth, but you shall meditate on it day and night, that you may observe to do all that is written in it. For then you will make your way prosperous, and then you will have good success."

Joshua is told this as he is about to lead God's people into the promised land. The promise is not that following God's Word will remove all difficulty. It is that meditating on it and doing it will make your way prosperous and give you good success. This applies to marriage: God has given you His Word on this matter. The invitation now is not to understand it but to live it.

Core Truth

The central idea of this lesson

You have learned the foundations about marriage well. Now the question is not whether you understand it, but whether you will live it.

From Foundation to Life: Twelve Lessons Become One Direction

In the course of twelve lessons, you have covered the ground God has given on marriage: why He created it, how to prepare for it, how to live inside it, how to handle the hardest parts of it, and how to protect it. You have heard from the opening chapters of Genesis to the intimate poetry of Song of Solomon to the hard-won wisdom of Paul and Peter. You have covered the landscape. What happens now is not the next lesson. It is the rest of your life living out these lessons.

This is where the real work begins, not because the teachings are difficult to understand — they are clear — but because living them consistently requires something deeper than intellectual agreement. It requires you to believe that God's Word on marriage is good, and true, and worth the cost of obedience.

Note

Every person in this series comes to it in a different place. Some are still single, testing these truths before they marry. Some are newly married, building a foundation. Some are in the middle years, either thriving or weathering difficulty. Some are wondering whether what God says about marriage can possibly be true in their circumstance. Whatever your place, the invitation is the same: take what you have heard and let it shape how you live.

A Coaching Question for Your Reflection

As you finish this series, consider these coaching questions. They are not for a final grade or a perfect answer. They are for you to know where you are and where the Spirit may be calling you to move.

Lesson 1: Why Marriage Exists. Do you believe marriage is God's idea? Does that belief change how you would approach it — whether you are about to enter it or you are already inside it?

Lesson 2: One Flesh. Have you understood that marriage is not primarily an emotional arrangement or a legal contract? It is a covenant that joins two people into one new reality. If that is true, what does that say about how you should treat it?

Lesson 3: How to Wait. Whether you are still waiting or you have already married, do you believe that waiting for marriage — staying pure and guarding your sexuality — is not a burden but a gift? That God is not depriving you but protecting you?

Lesson 4: How to Discern. If you are not yet married, are you asking the right questions about who you are considering? Are you looking for character, maturity and alignment, or are you hoping love will overcome fundamental differences?

Lesson 5: What to Know. As you approach marriage or as you live inside it, are you growing in knowledge of your spouse? Are you genuinely curious, or are you assuming you already know them?

Lesson 6: Biblical Roles. Do you understand that roles in marriage are not about power but about order? That there is dignity in both submission and headship, and that real strength shows itself through sacrificial love?

Lesson 7: How to Disagree. When conflict comes to your marriage, do you know how to fight in a way that protects rather than destroys? Are you staying in the hard conversation rather than running from it or weaponising it?

Lesson 8: Intimacy. Are you stewarding the physical and sexual dimension of your marriage as a sacred space? Are you protecting that intimacy as one of marriage's deepest privileges?

Lesson 9: Priorities. Have you put first things first? Is your marriage receiving your genuine attention and investment, or just what is left over?

Lesson 10: Guarding Your Heart. Do you understand the quiet dangers, the little foxes that spoil the vine? Are you actively protecting your heart and your marriage from the conditions that create vulnerability?

Lesson 11: Being Unequally Yoked. If you are considering marriage, have you taken seriously the call to marry only within the faith? If you are already unequally yoked, have you claimed God's grace and understood that your covenant covers your home?

Going Deeper

These questions are not meant to condemn but to clarify. If you have heard something in this series that you know you have not been living, that is not a reason for shame. That is a reason for change. The same God who has given you these truths has also given you grace, and power, and the Holy Spirit to walk in them. Repentance is simply turning from one direction toward another. If you hear yourself in any of these questions and realise you need to change, the time to change is now.

What Happens Tomorrow?

This series ends. Life continues. The invitations God has made in these twelve lessons will not fade, but they will not enforce themselves either. You are responsible for taking what you have heard and building it into the actual decisions, habits and directions of your marriage.

Consider three commitments as you leave this series and step back into your real life:

First: Share what you have learned. The person who most needs to hear this may not be you. If you have someone in your life — a friend, a young person, a couple you know — who needs these foundations, do not keep them to yourself. Recommend this series. Talk about what shifted for you. Let what you have learned serve others.

Second: Return to these lessons. Do not assume one listening is enough. Come back. Revisit the lessons that addressed your hardest questions. Read the passages. Meditate on them. Let Scripture do its slow and deep work on your heart across years, not just in one series.

Third: Live what you have learned. This is the only one that matters. Understanding marriage God's way is a good thing. Living it out is everything. Make the hard choice to prioritise your spouse. Have the difficult conversation. Guard the vulnerable space. Keep the covenant. Break the pattern. Love sacrificially. This is how the truth becomes real.

Practical Tip

Before you leave this series, pick one lesson that spoke most deeply to you. Pick one teaching, one insight, one question that stirred something in you. Write it down. Tell your spouse what it was. Make one specific change this week that flows from what you learned. Do not wait. The time to live this is not later. It is now.

What is the foundation of all twelve lessons in this series?

Marriage was God's idea, not ours. He created it, He understands it, and He has provided everything needed to make it work. All twelve lessons flow from this single truth.

What is the most important thing this series teaches about approaching or building marriage?

Marriage is a covenant, not a contract. It is not a feeling, a partnership, or an arrangement. It is a binding commitment before God that joins two people into one flesh. Treat it accordingly.

What did you learn about character and discernment before marriage?

Choose a spouse based on character, maturity, alignment with your faith and values, and shared direction — not on attraction or the feeling of being in love. Ask the hard questions. Look for someone who is growing toward Christ.

What is the key to a thriving marriage according to these lessons?

Investment: genuine attention, time, sacrifice, communication, physical intimacy, and putting first things first. A marriage does not thrive on assumption. It thrives on consistent, active, intentional love.

What did this series teach about protection and danger in marriage?

The greatest dangers are rarely dramatic. They accumulate quietly in unguarded spaces — emotional affairs, priority drift, neglect. Guard your heart actively. Tend your vine. Know the little foxes and catch them before they spoil the vineyard.

What is the final word of this entire series on marriage?

You have learned the foundation well. Now the question is not whether you understand it, but whether you will live it. Take what you have heard and build it into your actual choices and habits. The time to live this is now.

Bible Pointers on Marriage: Series Complete

You have walked through twelve lessons on marriage: why it exists, how to prepare for it, how to live inside it, and how to protect it. Now the work begins. Not the work of understanding — you have done that. The work of living it out. Take these truths into your marriage. Let them shape your choices. Return to them across the years. God has given you a good gift in marriage, and He has given you His Word to make it work. Go and build.
